Building AGI: The Engineering Path Beyond Transformers

Why scaling models isn't enough, and how novel geometric architectures are paving the way for next-generation intelligence.

For the past decade, we've treated Transformers as the holy grail of machine learning. The "Attention is All You Need" paper revolutionized how we approach sequence modeling, leading to the LLM era. But as we push the boundaries of reasoning, a harsh reality is setting in: attention scales quadratically, and throwing more compute at it yields diminishing returns for complex logic.

It's time to talk about the post-LLM architectures. We're moving from statistical parrots to topological reasoning engines. Let's break down how state space models (SSMs) and geometric deep learning are fundamentally changing the blueprint of intelligence.

The Architecture of Intelligence

Current models operate in flat vector spaces. They understand relationships through dot products. But reality isn't flat—it's highly structured, relational, and geometric. When we graph molecular structures or map social networks, we aren't dealing with sequences; we're dealing with graphs.

"We are trying to build 3D intelligence using 1D tools. The next breakthrough won't be a larger model, it will be a different geometry of processing."
